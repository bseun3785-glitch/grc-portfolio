Full policy text

1. Purpose

This policy defines the minimum requirements for creating, managing, and protecting passwords and other authentication credentials used to access company systems, applications, and data. Its purpose is to reduce the risk of unauthorized access resulting from weak, reused, shared, or compromised credentials, consistent with NIST SP 800-63B digital identity guidelines and the NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0 Protect function.

2. Scope

This policy applies to all employees, contractors, interns, and third-party vendors who are issued credentials to access company systems, including but not limited to email, cloud platforms, internal applications, administrative accounts, and customer-facing systems. It applies to all devices used to access company resources, whether company-issued or personal (BYOD).

3. Password Requirements

All passwords used to access company systems must meet the following minimum requirements:

  • Minimum length of 12 characters for standard user accounts, and 16 characters for privileged or administrative accounts.
  • A mix of uppercase and lowercase letters, numbers, and at least one special character, unless the system supports passphrase-based authentication of equivalent strength.
  • Passwords must not contain the user's name, username, or easily guessable personal information.
  • Passwords must not reuse any of the user's last 6 previous passwords.
  • Default or vendor-supplied passwords must be changed before a system or account is put into use.

4. Password Rotation

Standard user passwords must be changed at least every 180 days, or immediately upon any suspected or confirmed compromise. Privileged and administrative account passwords must be changed every 90 days. Where supported, systems should favor risk-based or compromise-triggered password changes over mandatory periodic rotation, consistent with current NIST guidance, provided multi-factor authentication (Section 5) is enforced.

5. Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

Multi-factor authentication is required for:

  • All remote access to company systems, including VPN and cloud applications.
  • All administrative and privileged accounts, without exception.
  • All access to systems storing or processing sensitive data, including customer personal information (PII) and financial records.
  • Where available, app-based or hardware-token MFA is preferred over SMS-based verification due to its stronger resistance to interception.

6. Account Lockout

Accounts must be automatically locked after 5 consecutive failed login attempts within a 15-minute window. Locked accounts require identity verification through IT Security or an approved self-service recovery process before access is restored. Repeated lockouts on the same account must be logged and reviewed for signs of a brute-force attempt.

7. Password Storage and Handling

  • Passwords must never be written down, stored in plain text files, or shared via email, chat, or messaging applications.
  • Employees must use the company-approved password manager to store and generate credentials.
  • Passwords must never be shared between individuals, including supervisors and direct reports, or between personal and work accounts.
  • System and service account passwords must be stored in a secure, access-controlled credential vault, not hardcoded into scripts or configuration files.

8. Roles and Responsibilities

Employees are responsible for safeguarding their own credentials and reporting any suspected compromise immediately to IT Security. IT Security is responsible for enforcing technical controls described in this policy, monitoring for policy violations, and reviewing exceptions. People Managers are responsible for ensuring their direct reports understand and comply with this policy as part of onboarding and ongoing security awareness training.

9. Enforcement and Exceptions

Violation of this policy may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination of employment or contract, consistent with company HR policy. Any exception to this policy must be documented, justified by business need, time-limited, and formally approved by IT Security leadership.

10. References

  • NIST Special Publication 800-63B, Digital Identity Guidelines: Authentication and Lifecycle Management
  • NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0, Protect (PR.AA): Identity Management, Authentication and Access Control

Project 4

Employee Onboarding Security Plan

Secure access and security awareness from the first day

Plan documentNIST CSF 2.0 Govern, ProtectSky Vault Calgary
Impact

Built a least-privilege onboarding framework with a role-based access matrix for 6 roles, targeting 100% MFA on Day 1, 100% training completion before client file access, and 100% offboarding within 24 hours.

When a new person joins, the firm decides what they can see and what they have been taught. Done well, this prevents several of the risks in the register. This plan sets out how Sky Vault Calgary gives new hires the access they need for their role, and no more, and makes sure they are trained before they touch client data.

Onboarding timeline

  1. Before day 1HR, Manager, IT

    Manager submits the onboarding request with role, manager and start date. Signed confidentiality agreement and acceptable use acknowledgment collected. IT prepares the account, disabled until day 1, with baseline access only.

  2. Day 1IT

    Identity checked in person. Account activated. Password created under the Corporate Password Policy (Project 3) and multi-factor authentication enrolled. Password manager set up. Firm laptop issued with disk encryption on.

  3. Days 1 to 5HR, New hire

    New hire completes Security Awareness Training (Project 2). Access to client files is granted only after completion is recorded.

  4. Day 30Manager, HR

    Manager reviews access against the role table below and removes anything not needed. HR files the training record and signed acknowledgments.

  5. Every quarterManagers, IT

    Managers confirm each person's access is still correct for their role.

  6. Every yearHR, IT

    Refresher training for all staff and review of this plan.

2. Priya Sandhu, HR Manager

2.1Walk me through what happens, step by step, when someone joins the firm and when someone leaves. 2.2What security and privacy training do staff receive, how often, and how do you know each person completed it?Why we ask: Shows whether staff are prepared for phishing and for handling client data, and whether completion is recorded as audit evidence.PR.AT

3.1How do staff sign in to email, the accounting software and file storage, and is multi-factor authentication required everywhere, including for administrator accounts?Why we ask: Weak or shared sign-in is a likely route to client data. This tests access control as it works in practice.PR.AA
3.2How are firm and client data backed up, and when did you last restore from a backup to prove it works?Why we ask: A backup that has never been restored may not work. This tests whether the firm can recover after an incident.PR.DSRC.RP
