About this project

A complete risk assessment for a growing SaaS company

I acted as the GRC Analyst for a 250-person Canadian software company and carried out a full security and privacy risk assessment, the way it would run in a real engagement.

Because the company is fictional, the evidence that control owners returned is simulated, with a realistic mix of complete, partial and missing responses. Every risk, control and action traces back to the same 14 assets, so the whole assessment reads as one connected piece of work.

The client

About Sandymichodrian Software Inc. (fictional)

Makes PineDesk, a customer-engagement SaaS platform used by 2,000 business customers (about 1.8 million end-contact records).

  • Toronto, Ontario, with a second office in Montréal, Québec
  • 250 employees and a 3-person security team
  • AWS, Salesforce, Workday, Okta, Google Workspace, GitHub and Zendesk
  • Subject to PIPEDA and Québec Law 25; customers ask for SOC 2

Impact

Mapped all 16 risks and 19 controls to NIST CSF 2.0 subcategories, with CIS Controls v8 safeguards as references, so progress can be reported to leadership by function.

Each risk has one primary CSF 2.0 function and the subcategories that describe the outcome it needs. Each control names the risks it treats, its owner and the CIS safeguard it follows.

What this tells leadership. Detect, Respond and Recover are the weakest functions. The company can protect data reasonably well, but it would be slow to notice an attack and has never proven it can recover.

Steps 8 and 9

Remediation Roadmap and Risk Prioritization

A costed 12-month plan and where each risk lands afterwards

21 actions4 phasesCosts in CAD
Impact

Built a 21-action plan costing about $136,000 one-off and $228,000 a year. No Critical or High risks remain afterwards, and the total risk score falls from 215 to 96, a 55% reduction.

Every action has an owner, a due date, a cost and a measure of success. The plan starts with quick wins that cost almost nothing, which builds support for the larger investments that follow.

Four phases

  1. Phase 1 · 0-30 days5 actions

    Quick wins at almost no cost. Removes the only Critical risk.

    One-off: $12,000  |  Annual: $18,000

  2. Phase 2 · 30-90 days6 actions

    Detection and recovery: 24x7 monitoring, immutable backups, patching SLAs.

    One-off: $44,000  |  Annual: $133,000

  3. Phase 3 · 90-180 days7 actions

    Stronger identity and privacy: phishing-resistant MFA, PIAs, data retention, DR testing.

    One-off: $70,000  |  Annual: $39,000

  4. Phase 4 · 180-365 days3 actions

    Mature the program: full policy set, DLP alerts, regular phishing simulations.

Reflection

What I learned

  • Evidence changes the picture. Several controls looked fine on paper, but the evidence showed gaps, such as 11 active accounts for people who had left.

  • Privacy law shapes security priorities. PIPEDA and Québec Law 25 turn missing records and assessments into regulatory risk, not only good practice.

  • Small teams need leverage. With only 3 security staff, automation and an MDR partner reduce more risk than adding more manual tasks.

  • Quick wins build momentum. Phase 1 removes the only Critical risk at almost no cost, which helps win support for the larger investments.

  • Lead with the answer. Management needs the bottom line, the cost and the decision required, with the detail available in the register.
